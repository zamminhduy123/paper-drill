1. Cross-Dataset Generalization Splits (Train on One, Test on Another)

Since the thesis focuses on deep learning for in-vehicle network intrusion detection, the most natural splits test whether models generalize across vehicle platforms and attack distributions.

T3: Car-Hacking early → late      First 60% of recorded attack captures  Next 15%              Final 25%              Test temporal stability on Car-Hacking

7. Recommended Primary Split for the Thesis

Given the thesis statement and the available datasets, the most defensible primary split is:

Primary Split (P1):

* Train: Car-Hacking + ROAD + CAN-ML (combined, with 15% held out for validation)

* Validation: Held-out 15% from each of Car-Hacking, ROAD, and CAN-ML

* Test: CIC-IoV-2024 (entirely unseen dataset)

This directly tests the thesis claim that deep learning methods can be applied to in-vehicle network areas and generalize to unseen IoV data, using only the four permitted datasets.

Secondary Split (P2):

* Train: Car-Hacking + ROAD

* Validation: Held-out 15% of Car-Hacking + ROAD

* Test: CAN-ML + CIC-IoV-2024

This provides a second cross-domain evaluation with a different source/target configuration.
