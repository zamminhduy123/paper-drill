Transfer Hypotheses Across IVN, IoT-IDS, and NIDS

* Author-stated: The thesis is to "Transfer proven methods across network intrusion domains (ivn, iot-ids, nids): apply Method X from one domain to Problem Y in another."

* Inference (model's): The scope notes collectively identify recurring failure modes — dataset-specific correlations, non-exchangeability, class imbalance, offline-only evaluation, and narrow validated envelopes — that are candidate targets for cross-domain method transfer.

* Proposed experiment: Instantiate each Method X → Problem Y below, then run the paired datasets in the specified splits.

Cross-Domain Transfer Proposals

IVN → IoT-IDS and NIDS

1. Temporal-convolutional-network check (IVN) → streaming/on-device detection (IoT-IDS).

+ Author-stated (IVN): "The temporal-convolutional-network check is bounded and does not exhaustively cover detector architectures or deployment conditions."

+ Inference (model's): A TCN pretrained on CAN windows may transfer to IoT flow windows if temporal locality dominates over protocol semantics.

+ Proposed experiment: Pretrain a TCN on Car-Hacking and CAN-ML; fine-tune on N-BaIoT and TON_IoT; test on CIC-IoT-2023.

2. CAN IDS cross-dataset evaluation protocol (IVN) → standardized benchmarking (NIDS).

+ Author-stated (IVN): "Evaluation of CAN IDS methods remains difficult due to inconsistencies in experimental setups and the lack of standardized benchmarking frameworks."

+ Inference (model's): A shared protocol with fixed splits, seeds, and cost reporting can be ported from CAN to NIDS to expose dataset-dependency.

+ Proposed experiment: Apply one protocol across CIC-IDS-2017, UNSW-NB15, CIC-DDoS-2019, and CIC-IoT-2023 with identical preprocessing and report per-class metrics.

3. Cross-dataset generalization diagnostics (IVN) → environment-specific artifact removal (IoT-IDS/NIDS).

+ Author-stated (IVN): "The framework may not fully resolve environment-specific artifacts in security datasets that cause models to rely on dataset-specific correlations rather than security-relevant features."

+ Inference (model's): Artifact probes developed for CAN may reveal shortcut features in IoT and NIDS datasets.

+ Proposed experiment: Train on CIC-IDS-2017, test on UNSW-NB15 and CIC-DDoS-2019, with and without artifact-suppression layers.

IoT-IDS → IVN and NIDS

1. SHAP-based interpretability (IoT-IDS) → minority-class auditing (NIDS).

+ Author-stated (IoT-IDS): "SHAP explanations add interpretability but not causal validation."

+ Author-stated (NIDS): "Rare attack types (probing, brute force, cryptomining) are difficult to detect due to class imbalance."

+ Inference (model's): SHAP can localize which features drive rare-class errors, but cannot establish causality.

+ Proposed experiment: Train on CIC-IDS-2017 and UNSW-NB15; compute SHAP per rare class; compare against a causal-invariance baseline on CIC-DDoS-2019.

2. Edge-deployment cost profiling (IoT-IDS) → IVN real-world deployment gap.

+ Author-stated (IoT-IDS): "evaluation is offline and benchmark-based, so on-device deployment, streaming traffic, and real hardware constraints are not demonstrated."

+ Author-stated (IVN): "real-world deployment trials not yet conducted."

+ Inference (model's): Latency/energy profiling methods from IoT edge can be reused for in-vehicle CAN IDS.

+ Proposed experiment: Measure latency and energy for a detector on Car-Hacking and CAN-ML under streaming simulation; replicate the same harness on N-BaIoT.

3. Quantized LLM vs classical detector cost comparison (IoT-IDS) → NIDS baseline discipline.

+ Author-stated (IoT-IDS): "classical detectors match or exceed quantized LLM accuracy at one to three orders of magnitude lower cost on the same device."

+ Author-stated (NIDS): "evaluation datasets, baselines, and computational cost are unspecified."

+ Inference (model's): Mandatory classical baselines with cost reporting would tighten NIDS claims.

+ Proposed experiment: On CIC-IDS-2017, UNSW-NB15, and CIC-DDoS-2019, report accuracy, F1, latency, and energy for classical vs neural detectors.

NIDS → IVN and IoT-IDS

1. Conformal prediction under non-exchangeability (NIDS) → IVN CAN scheduling.

+ Author-stated (NIDS): "exchangeability violation under shift causes 100% miscoverage."

+ Author-stated (IVN): "IVN CAN bus traffic violates exchangeability due to deterministic scheduling and physical constraints."

+ Inference (model's): Conformal methods must be adapted (e.g., weighted or temporal conformal) before use on CAN.

+ Proposed experiment: Apply conformal detectors to Car-Hacking and CAN-ML; measure marginal vs conditional coverage across attack types.

2. Imbalance-aware pipeline (NIDS) → IoT-IDS rare-attack detection.

+ Author-stated (NIDS): "High-dimensional and imbalanced data like ALLFLOWMETER_HIKARI2021 leads to poor classification and minority attack detection."

+ Author-stated (IoT-IDS): "findings may not generalize beyond the three tested IoT datasets."

+ Inference (model's): Imbalance techniques may transfer, but dataset-specific tuning is likely required.

+ Proposed experiment: Train imbalance-aware models on TON_IoT and N-BaIoT; test on CIC-IoT-2023.

3. TRMI-FCM overhead analysis (NIDS) → IoT-IDS edge feasibility.

+ Author-stated (NIDS): "TRMI-FCM introduces FCM construction and MI computation overhead whose scalability to higher-dimensional or streaming traffic is not addressed."

+ Author-stated (IoT-IDS): "scalability, real-time performance, edge-device feasibility, and operational effectiveness require further experimental validation."

+ Inference (model's): The overhead concern is shared across both domains.

+ Proposed experiment: Benchmark TRMI-FCM-style feature construction on N-BaIoT, TON_IoT, and CIC-IoT-2023 under streaming constraints.

Claims Audit

* Author-stated (IVN): "lower-frequency attacks not addressed."

* Author-stated (IoT-IDS): "results rest on a single offline dataset with no deployment, latency, or resource measurements."

* Author-stated (NIDS): "Validation is limited to a single dataset and single train-test split, so cross-dataset generalization and temporal stability are untested."
