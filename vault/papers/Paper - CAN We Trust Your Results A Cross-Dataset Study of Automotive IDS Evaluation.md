# CAN We Trust Your Results? A Cross-Dataset Study of Automotive IDS Evaluation

Novelty

The paper introduces a benchmarking framework for consistent, cross-dataset evaluation of CAN intrusion detection systems. Unlike prior benchmark studies that evaluate multiple IDS approaches on a single dataset, this work integrates seven publicly available CAN IDS datasets collected under different experimental conditions and performs cross-dataset evaluation of five conceptually different IDS approaches. It explicitly supports cross-dataset evaluation while simultaneously considering multiple IDS categories, attack families, and datasets (see Table 1). The work also documents a case where inconsistencies in published performance metrics stemmed from incorrect interpretation of evaluation outputs, requiring re-implementation and recomputation to detect.

Methodology

The authors propose a unified benchmarking framework standardizing dataset representation, evaluation procedures, and IDS integration for reproducible experimentation. They integrate seven publicly available CAN IDS datasets with different attack scenarios and vehicle environments into a common representation. A standardized evaluation workflow is provided to enable fair comparison between IDS methods with different detection principles. Using this framework, they perform cross-dataset evaluation on five conceptually different methods to study generalization capabilities across multiple datasets and attack families. The framework addresses dataset bias and data drift, including covariate shift from differences in vehicle platforms, ECU configurations, or driving conditions, and label distribution changes from differing attack type proportions or implementations.

Explicit Limitations

Evaluation of CAN IDS methods remains difficult due to inconsistencies in experimental setups and the lack of standardized benchmarking frameworks. Reported performance often depends on dataset-specific characteristics and may not reflect how detection methods behave in different environments. Many studies rely on proprietary datasets that are not publicly available, or use datasets collected under different experimental conditions, making results difficult to reproduce or compare directly. Reproduction of prior work is further complicated by limited availability of implementation details or source code. Most existing benchmark studies focus on testing multiple IDS approaches on a single dataset, which does not reveal whether detection methods generalize across different vehicle environments, attack scenarios, or data collection setups. Security datasets often contain environment-specific artifacts introduced during data collection, preprocessing, or labeling, which learning algorithms may rely on instead of capturing underlying malicious behavior, and even simple or unintended baselines can perform surprisingly well when such artifacts are present. IDS performance may degrade when deployed in environments different from those used during development, a phenomenon associated with dataset bias and data drift.

One transfer to IVN: the cross-dataset evaluation methodology addresses whether CAN IDS methods generalize across different vehicle environments, attack scenarios, and data collection setups, directly relevant to in-vehicle network security.
