# Detection Depth and Distributed Trust in AI-Based IoT Intrusion Detection: A Systematic Analysis

## Novelty
The paper provides a systematic analysis of blockchain-integrated deep learning intrusion detection for IoT, focusing on the underexamined relationship between detection depth and blockchain trust scope. It introduces a three-tier protocol-awareness taxonomy (L1 flow-level, L2 protocol-field, L3 protocol-state/semantic) and identifies a dependency chain among methodological, empirical, and knowledge gaps.

## Methodology
The study codes 50 published studies against seven dimensions: protocol-awareness level, blockchain role, blockchain integration depth, consensus mechanism, federated learning use, deployment domain, and study quality. It applies the three-tier PA-LEVEL taxonomy and maps identified gaps to an established gap taxonomy.

## Explicit Limitations
Not stated for the analysis itself. The abstract identifies field-level limitations: dominant datasets lack IoT representativeness, blockchain trust properties are often asserted without benchmarking, and no study co-designs detection depth and trust scope from a shared threat model.

## Future Work
The proposed research agenda resolves the gaps in sequence: improve IoT-representative evaluation datasets, benchmark blockchain trust properties empirically, and co-design detection depth and trust scope as jointly constrained variables derived from a shared threat model.
